# 03 · Product Copy Framework + Examples

## The PC Era product description formula
Sell a *feeling*, not a spec sheet. Every product page follows this order:

1. **The era hook** (1 line, italic-worthy): *"In your vacation era."*
2. **The story** (2–3 sentences): where she wears it, how it makes her feel.
3. **Why you'll love it** (3 bullet-style lines — fit, fabric, versatility).
4. **Made for every era** (the cross-era styling line — this piece across 2–3 eras).
5. **The details** (fabric, fit, model, care — in accordions, not the hero copy).

Tone: editorial, sensory, confident. Mention fabric as a feeling ("buttery", "weightless", "structured") before naming it.

## Example 1 — *The Amalfi Linen Set*
**Era hook:** In your vacation era.
**Story:** Salt in your hair, espresso on the table, nowhere to be. The Amalfi Set is the one you'll live in from the airport to the aperitivo — relaxed tailoring in breathable European linen that only looks better a little crumpled.
**Why you'll love it:**
- A relaxed, elevated fit that skims (never clings).
- Premium 100% European linen — weightless and breathable.
- Wears three ways: together as a set, or split across your whole wardrobe.
**Made for every era:** Together for *vacation era*. Blazer-over-tee for *brunch era*. Trousers with heels for *date night era*.

## Example 2 — *The Main Character Maxi*
**Era hook:** In your main character era.
**Story:** Some dresses you wear; this one you arrive in. A column silhouette in liquid satin that moves when you do — built for the moment everyone quietly turns to look.
**Why you'll love it:**
- A bias cut that flatters every body and lengthens the line.
- Liquid-satin drape with a cool, expensive hand.
- Occasion-ready, but too good to save for one.
**Made for every era:** *Date night era* with a slicked bun. *Main character era* with gold and a blow-dry. *Confident era* under a sharp blazer.

**Made for every era:** *Soft girl era* barefoot at home. *Brunch era* with sandals. *Healing era* with an oversized cardigan.
